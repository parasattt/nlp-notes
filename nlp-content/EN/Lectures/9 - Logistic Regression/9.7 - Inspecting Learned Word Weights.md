## Inspect what the learned weights favour

Sort the learned weights and keep the corresponding feature names aligned. The smallest weights push toward negative; the largest push toward positive. This lets us compare learned evidence with the hand-written sentiment list.



```python
vocab = tfidf.get_feature_names_out()
order = w.argsort()

print("most negative weights:", list(vocab[order[:8]]))
print("most positive weights:", list(vocab[order[-8:]][::-1]))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
most negative weights: ['after', 'disappointed', 'cheap', 'poor', 'broke', 'not', 'stopped', 'unbearable']
most positive weights: ['for', 'well', 'good', 'excellent', 'my', 'exactly', 'love', 'on']
```



Many words fit expectations: `disappointed`, `cheap`, `poor`, `broke`, and `not` are negative; `well`, `good`, `excellent`, and `love` are positive.

`after` is more interesting. It was not on the hand-written sentiment list, but complaints repeatedly use it in phrases such as `broke after two days` and `stopped working after a week`. The model can learn useful patterns that a hand-written list misses.

The positive words `for`, `my`, and `on` are less convincing. Phrases such as `for the price` and `for my parents` may explain them in this collection. With only 90 training reviews, some weights can reflect useful patterns and others accidental associations. The weights alone cannot establish which is which.
