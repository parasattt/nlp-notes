## Keep preprocessing and classification in a pipeline

Fitting a vectorizer on all the text would let test vocabulary leak into training. A **Pipeline** puts the vectorizer and classifier into one workflow. Calling `fit` on the training texts fits both steps using that training data. Prediction on new texts only transforms with the fitted vectorizer before using the fitted classifier.

`make_pipeline(TfidfVectorizer(), LogisticRegression(max_iter=2000))` therefore accepts raw review text and returns labels or probabilities. `max_iter` sets the optimiser's allowed iteration limit; it is not a hand-chosen word weight.

Compare the manual boundary in [[EN/Lectures/7 - Naive Bayes Classification/7.7 - Train-Test Splits and Data Leakage|Train-Test Splits and Data Leakage]].



```python
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline

model = make_pipeline(TfidfVectorizer(), LogisticRegression(max_iter=2000))
model.fit(tr_txt, y_tr)

print("test accuracy:", round(model.score(te_txt, y_te), 3))
print()
for t in ["The battery died after two weeks!!", "Fits perfectly and looks elegant"]:
    p = model.predict_proba([t])[0, 1]
    print(f"P(positive) = {p:.2f}  | {t}")
```


**Saved output:**

```text
test accuracy: 0.6

P(positive) = 0.35  | The battery died after two weeks!!
P(positive) = 0.58  | Fits perfectly and looks elegant
```



The saved pipeline has test accuracy 0.6. It gives positive probabilities 0.35 for the battery complaint and 0.58 for the praise about fit and appearance.

The complete text-classification workflow can now be fitted and used through the same pipeline: raw text enters and a probability comes out.
