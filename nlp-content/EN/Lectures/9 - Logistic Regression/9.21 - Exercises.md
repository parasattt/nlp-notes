## Exercises

### 1. Reconstruct one prediction from its features

Use `lr_fit` from the 120-review coefficient section and the review `"The battery died after two weeks!!"`.

1. Get the TF-IDF vector with `tf_step.transform(...)`.
2. Compute its dot product with `lr_step.coef_[0]`, then add `lr_step.intercept_[0]`.
3. Apply `sigmoid`. Check that the result matches `lr_fit.predict_proba(...)`.
4. Find the word with the largest contribution, calculated as weight times feature value. Is it the word you expected?

### 2. Set a support threshold for a 90% recall rule

The shop now requires at least 90% of negative reviews to reach support.

1. From the earlier out-of-fold `p_negative`, find the **highest** threshold that still meets this rule.
2. Report the total number of reviews sent to support and the fraction of that queue that is actually positive.
3. Do the same with the Amazon test probabilities `p_neg_big`. How many of 5,000 reviews must be read to catch 90% of the complaints? Compare the wasted share with the 120-review case.
4. Would you trust the chosen threshold next month? Discuss how many negative reviews the 90% recall was measured on in each dataset.

### 3. Find where model performance crosses

The large-data source results put NB ahead at 96 reviews and LR ahead at 20,000.

1. Repeat the intended large-data experiment at sizes between 100 and 2,000, for example `[100, 200, 300, 500, 750, 1000, 1500, 2000]`. Find roughly where LR + TF-IDF overtakes NB + counts.
2. Use three different `random_state` values in `.sample(...)`. Does the crossing point move, and by how much?
3. Add `NB + tf-idf` to `big_setups`. Does your evidence connect the gap to the classifier, the representation, or both? Explain using the numbers.
4. Inspect the 20,000-review NB model's most confident test mistakes, as in the earlier confidence comparison. Does overconfidence improve or worsen with more data?
