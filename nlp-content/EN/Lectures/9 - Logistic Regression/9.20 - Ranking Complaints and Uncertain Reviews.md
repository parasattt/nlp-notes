## Use probabilities to order the reading queue

The team lead needs a reading order, so the next cell sorts the 5,000 held-out reviews by the stored large model's negative probability. It prints two useful groups: the five most likely complaints and the five reviews closest to probability 0.5.

The first group belongs at the front of a complaint queue. The second group is uncertain, rather than simply positive: these reviews may need a person's judgement. The displayed results were saved in the notebook; the earlier loading and fitting errors were not repaired or rerun during conversion.



The code uses negative-probability column 0, sorts descending for the complaint queue, and separately sorts distance from 0.5 for the uncertain queue. Labels are displayed for inspection of the stored results.

```python
p_neg_big = big_lr.predict_proba(a_test["text"])[:, 0]
order_big = p_neg_big.argsort()[::-1]

print("READ FIRST (most likely complaints):")
for i in order_big[:5]:
    print(f"  {p_neg_big[i]:.3f}  [{a_test['sentiment'].iloc[i]}]  {a_test['text'].iloc[i][:100]}...")
print()
print("UNSURE (closest to 0.5):")
for i in np.abs(p_neg_big - 0.5).argsort()[:5]:
    print(f"  {p_neg_big[i]:.3f}  [{a_test['sentiment'].iloc[i]}]  {a_test['text'].iloc[i][:100]}...")
```

Identifying uncertainty gives the human reader another useful queue, alongside the most likely complaints.
