## Recap

- A word can have many surface forms that string comparison treats as different.
- **Stemming** removes affixes using rules. It is fast, needs no dictionary and often produces strings that are not full words. Over-stemming can merge `university` and `universe`.
- **Lemmatization** uses dictionary information and handles changes such as `mice -> mouse`. Supply POS when needed; otherwise it may quietly give a poor result, as the saved `was -> wa` noun example shows.
- `spaCy` combines tokenization, POS and lemmas in one call. Its saved `better -> well` differs from the WordNet example's `better -> good`.
- For Russian, the lecture uses Snowball for stems and `pymorphy3` for lemmas. `parse()` returns ranked readings because forms such as `стали` are ambiguous.
- In the lecture's Kazakh tooling comparison, there is no packaged lemmatizer. NLTK does not include a Turkish stemmer; the separate Turkish stemmer leaves these Cyrillic words unchanged; Russian rules damage some words. The exercise uses handmade suffix rules or subwords.
- Subwords sometimes align with Kazakh morphemes and sometimes split a root. The lecture values their ability to keep representing unfamiliar words, rather than treating the splits as linguistic analyses.
- Inspect the changed tokens. A call that runs successfully can still leave everything unchanged or damage the text.
