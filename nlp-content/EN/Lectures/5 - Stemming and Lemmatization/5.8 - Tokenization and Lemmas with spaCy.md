The loaded pipeline processes the same sentence as the manual POS example and prints the original token, lemma, POS and stop word flag.

```python
import spacy

nlp = spacy.load("en_core_web_sm")

doc = nlp("The striped bats were hanging on their feet and eating better mice")

print(f"{'token':12}{'lemma':12}{'POS':8}{'stop?'}")
print("-" * 38)
for t in doc:
    print(f"{t.text:12}{t.lemma_:12}{t.pos_:8}{t.is_stop}")
```

This is a shorter version of the hand-built pipeline, and it also reports stop word flags. `t.text` is the original token, `t.lemma_` its predicted lemma, `t.pos_` its POS, and `t.is_stop` its stop word flag.

**Source discrepancy:** the notebook calls this the “same result”, but the saved spaCy output gives `better -> well`; the earlier WordNet pipeline gives `better -> good`. The other displayed correspondences are similar. Keep the actual saved outputs when comparing tools.
