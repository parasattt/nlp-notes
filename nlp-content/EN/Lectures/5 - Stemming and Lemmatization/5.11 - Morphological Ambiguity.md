## Why parse() returns a list



For each ambiguous string, display the first three analyses, POS labels and saved scores. Repeated lemmas may have different grammatical readings.

```python
for w in ["стали", "мыла", "три"]:
    print(w)
    for p in morph.parse(w)[:3]:
        print(f"      {p.normal_form:12} {str(p.tag.POS):6} score={p.score:.2f}")
    print()
```

A written form can have several grammatical readings:

- `стали`: **стать** (“to become”) or **сталь** (“steel”).
- `мыла`: **мыло** (“soap”) or **мыть** (“to wash”).
- `три`: **три** (“three”) or **тереть** (“rub!”).

`morph.parse(w)` returns ranked alternatives. Choosing `[0]` takes the highest-ranked reading without checking the sentence. The lecture explains `score` as a probability based on the training corpus. It uses `мыла стали дороже` to show that picking the first reading can be wrong for at least one word.

The lecture describes `pymorphy3` as fast, offline and usually correct for Russian, while stressing that it can make mistakes.
