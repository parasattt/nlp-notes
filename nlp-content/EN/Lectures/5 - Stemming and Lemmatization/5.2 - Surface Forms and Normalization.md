The same word can appear in several forms. A computer comparing the strings treats them as different items:

| Related forms | A computer sees |
|---|---|
| run, runs, running, ran | 4 different strings |
| study, studies, studied, studying | 4 different strings |
| ноутбук, ноутбука, ноутбуку, ноутбуком | 4 different strings |
| кітап, кітабы, кітаптар, кітаптарымызда | 4 different strings |

Reducing related forms to one shared form is **normalization**. This lecture compares two methods that are often confused: stemming and lemmatization. They follow [[EN/Lectures/4 - Tokenization/4.1 - Tokens and Tokenization Decisions|tokenization]], which first divides text into units.

## Why normalize words?



Lowercase alphabetic tokens are counted without normalizing their forms. Compare the eight most common tokens with the text’s topics, and keep the saved distinct-token count of 28 as the baseline.

```python
from nltk.tokenize import word_tokenize

text = """The runner runs every morning. She ran ten kilometres yesterday and will run again
tomorrow. Running is her way of studying her own limits, and she has studied them for years."""

tokens = [w.lower() for w in word_tokenize(text) if w.isalpha()]

print(Counter(tokens).most_common(8))
print()
print("distinct tokens:", len(set(tokens)))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
[('she', 2), ('and', 2), ('her', 2), ('the', 1), ('runner', 1), ('runs', 1), ('every', 1), ('morning', 1)]

distinct tokens: 28
```



This text discusses **running** and **studying**, but `run`, `runs`, `ran` and `running` are counted separately. Neither topic reaches the top of the count list.

Methods based on word counts receive weaker evidence when related forms remain separate. This also affects the vocabulary used by [[EN/Lectures/6 - Turning Text into Features/6.2 - Vocabulary and Bag of Words|bag-of-words features]].
