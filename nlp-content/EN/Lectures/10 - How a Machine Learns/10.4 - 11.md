### A first guess, by hand

Guess $w = 12$, $b = 0$: "12 minutes per 100 words". For each job, the **error** (also called the
**residual**) is prediction minus truth, $\hat{y} - y$:

| $x$ | $y$ | $\hat{y} = 12x + 0$ | error $\hat{y} - y$ | $|$error$|$ | error² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12 | 12 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 21 | 24 | 3 | 3 | 9 |
| 3 | 33 | 36 | 3 | 3 | 9 |
| 4 | 41 | 48 | 7 | 7 | 49 |
| 5 | 52 | 60 | 8 | 8 | 64 |

Five errors are hard to compare between two guesses. A **loss** squeezes them into one number.
Two common choices:

- **MAE** (*mean absolute error*): the average of $|$error$|$, ignoring the sign:
  $(0 + 3 + 3 + 7 + 8) / 5 = $ **4.2 minutes**. Easy to explain to a client: "on average we are 4.2
  minutes off".
- **MSE** (*mean squared error*): the average of error²:
  $(0 + 9 + 9 + 49 + 64) / 5 = $ **26.2**.

$$\text{MSE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2$$

($\sum$, the Greek letter *sigma*, means "add up over all examples"; $n$ is the number of examples.)

Why square? Two reasons. Squaring punishes large errors much more than small ones: one error of 8
costs 64, while two errors of 4 cost only 16 + 16 = 32. And the square is a smooth curve without a
sharp corner at 0, which makes the slope easy to compute - needed in Part 4. Most regression models
are trained with MSE and reported with MAE, because MAE is in the original units (minutes, stars).

```python
def predict(x, w, b):
    return w * x + b

def mse(y_pred, y_true):
    return np.mean((y_pred - y_true) ** 2)

def mae(y_pred, y_true):
    return np.mean(np.abs(y_pred - y_true))

for w, b in [(12, 0), (10, 0), (10, 2)]:
    y_hat = predict(x, w, b)
    print(f"w = {w:2}, b = {b}:  predictions {y_hat}   MAE {mae(y_hat, y):.2f}   MSE {mse(y_hat, y):.2f}")
```

**Saved output:**

```text

w = 10, b = 0:  predictions [10. 20. 30. 40. 50.]   MAE 1.80   MSE 3.80
w = 10, b = 2:  predictions [12. 22. 32. 42. 52.]   MAE 0.60   MSE 0.60
```

$w = 10, b = 2$ is much better than the first guess. But guessing is not a method. We need a
procedure that finds the best $w$ and $b$ for any data.
