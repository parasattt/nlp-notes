# Part 3. The simplest model: a straight line

A translation agency wants to quote clients a time automatically. It has records of five finished
jobs:

| words (hundreds) $x$ | minutes $y$ |
|---|---|
| 1 | 12 |
| 2 | 21 |
| 3 | 33 |
| 4 | 41 |
| 5 | 52 |

The model: a straight line,

$$\hat{y} = w \cdot x + b$$

- $\hat{y}$ ("y-hat") is the **prediction**; $y$ without the hat is the true answer
- $w$ is the **weight**, here the slope: extra minutes per 100 extra words
- $b$ is the **bias** (also *intercept*): the minutes predicted for a text of length 0 - a fixed
  overhead such as reading the brief and sending the file

$w$ and $b$ are the model's two **parameters**. Learning means choosing them.

```python
x = np.array([1, 2, 3, 4, 5], dtype=float)       # hundreds of words
y = np.array([12, 21, 33, 41, 52], dtype=float)  # minutes

plt.figure(figsize=(4.5, 3.2))
plt.scatter(x, y, zorder=3)
plt.xlabel("words (hundreds)"); plt.ylabel("minutes")
plt.title("Five past translation jobs", fontsize=10)
plt.xlim(0, 6); plt.ylim(0, 60)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```
