## stop_words: the negation trap again

[[EN/Lectures/4 - Tokenization/4.6 - Preserving Negation|Removing negations during preprocessing]] can destroy the meaning of a sentence. The same failure can happen inside `CountVectorizer`.



First keep the default word vocabulary so the negative sentence retains its `not` feature.

```python
opinions = ["the product is good", "the product is not good"]

keep = CountVectorizer()
keep.fit(opinions)
print("keeping stop words :", keep.get_feature_names_out())
print(keep.transform(opinions).toarray())
```


**Saved output:**

```text
keeping stop words : ['good' 'is' 'not' 'product' 'the']
[[1 1 0 1 1]
 [1 1 1 1 1]]
```



Now fit a separate vectorizer with the built-in English stop list and inspect the lost distinction.

```python
drop = CountVectorizer(stop_words="english")
drop.fit(opinions)

print("dropping stop words:", drop.get_feature_names_out())
print(drop.transform(opinions).toarray())
```


**Saved output:**

```text
dropping stop words: ['good' 'product']
[[1 1]
 [1 1]]
```



With the default vocabulary, the two reviews have different vectors because `not` is present only in the negative sentence. After English stop word removal, both become exactly `[1, 1]` for `good` and `product`.

A classifier receiving only these identical vectors cannot recover which sentence was negative. The information is gone before prediction.

`sklearn`'s English list includes `not`, `no`, `nor`, `never` and `against`. For sentiment, reviews, complaints or any other task where negation matters, inspect the stop list before using it. The notebook also names medical notes as an example of meaning-sensitive text. The next cell shows all eight checked negation-related items in the saved list, whose total size is 318.



Check exactly which of the eight listed negation-related words the English stop list contains, then print its total size.

```python
from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import ENGLISH_STOP_WORDS

negations = sorted(w for w in ENGLISH_STOP_WORDS
                   if w in {"no", "not", "nor", "never", "none", "cannot", "without", "against"})
print(negations)
print()
print("stop list size:", len(ENGLISH_STOP_WORDS))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
['against', 'cannot', 'never', 'no', 'none', 'nor', 'not', 'without']

stop list size: 318
```
