## Every default is a choice

`CountVectorizer()` has settings even when you supply no arguments. It makes tokenization and normalization choices similar to those you previously made by hand. Each choice can remove information, so inspect the settings before relying on the output.



## lowercase: enabled by default



Fit one vocabulary with default lowercasing and another with `lowercase=False`, then compare their saved feature names.

```python
case_demo = ["Free money", "free money", "FREE MONEY"]

on = CountVectorizer()
on.fit(case_demo)

off = CountVectorizer(lowercase=False)
off.fit(case_demo)

print("lowercase=True  ->", on.get_feature_names_out())
print("lowercase=False ->", off.get_feature_names_out())
```


**Saved output:**

```text
lowercase=True  -> ['free' 'money']
lowercase=False -> ['FREE' 'Free' 'MONEY' 'free' 'money']
```



Lowercasing merges `Free`, `free` and `FREE` into the same word feature. This is often useful, but it also removes the capitalisation signal: `FREE MONEY` may look more like spam than `free money`.

The earlier [[EN/Lectures/3 - Parsing Text and Regex/3.12 - Extracting Features from Raw Text|raw-text feature examples]] measured signals such as shouting. Lowercasing the word representation discards that distinction. Decide whether that trade fits the task.
