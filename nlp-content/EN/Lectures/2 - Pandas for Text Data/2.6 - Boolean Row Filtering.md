### Filtering rows

Filtering keeps the rows that meet a condition. First create a column of `True` and `False` values, then use it inside `df[...]` to retain the `True` rows.



```python
# A comparison on a column gives a column of True/False
df["rating"] >= 4
```


**Saved output:**

```text
0      True
1     False
2      True
3     False
4      True
5     False
6      True
7     False
8      True
9     False
10    False
11     True
Name: rating, dtype: bool
```



```python
# Feed that back into the DataFrame to keep only the True rows
good = df[df["rating"] >= 4]
good
```


**Saved output:**

```text
    review_id product  rating                                                    review  verified
0           1  laptop       5  Excellent laptop, fast and quiet. Battery lasts all day.      True
2           3   phone       4               Good phone for the price, camera is decent.     False
4           5  tablet       5         Perfect for reading and taking notes. Very light.      True
6           7   phone       5    Best phone I have owned. Fast, great screen, worth it.      True
8           9  tablet       4        Nice tablet overall, though the speakers are weak.     False
11         12  tablet       5     Fantastic build quality and the display is beautiful.      True
```



```python
# Combine conditions with & (and) and | (or).
# Each condition MUST be wrapped in its own parentheses.
df[(df["rating"] >= 4) & (df["product"] == "phone")]
```


**Saved output:**

```text
   review_id product  rating                                                  review  verified
2          3   phone       4             Good phone for the price, camera is decent.     False
6          7   phone       5  Best phone I have owned. Fast, great screen, worth it.      True
```

`df["rating"] >= 4` creates a **boolean mask**, one decision per row. `df[mask]` keeps matching rows; six reviews meet this rating threshold in the saved data. Combining this with `product == "phone"` keeps the two highly rated phone reviews.



Two rules matter when combining column conditions:

- Use `&` for “and” and `|` for “or”. Python's `and` and `or` combine individual truth values; here you need to combine conditions across whole columns.
- Put **each condition inside parentheses**. Operators such as `&` have higher precedence than `==`, so missing parentheses can make Python interpret the expression differently and raise a confusing error.
