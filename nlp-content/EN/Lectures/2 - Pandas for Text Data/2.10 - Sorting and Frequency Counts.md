### Sorting and counting

Sorting helps inspect extremes, such as the longest reviews. Counting shows how often categories or ratings occur.



```python
df.sort_values("word_count", ascending=False).head(4)[["review", "word_count"]]
```


**Saved output:**

```text
                                                     review  word_count
6    Best phone I have owned. Fast, great screen, worth it.          10
0  Excellent laptop, fast and quiet. Battery lasts all day.           9
9       Overheats under any real load. Not worth the money.           9
1     Screen died after three weeks. Support was unhelpful.           8
```

`ascending=False` puts the largest word counts first. `.head(4)` keeps four rows, and the final double-bracket selection displays only review text and count. The longest saved review has **10 words**; the next two shown have **9**.



```python
df["product"].value_counts()      # how many rows per product
```


**Saved output:**

```text
product
laptop    4
phone     4
tablet    4
Name: count, dtype: int64
```



```python
df["rating"].value_counts().sort_index()   # distribution of ratings
```


**Saved output:**

```text
rating
1    2
2    2
3    2
4    2
5    4
Name: count, dtype: int64
```

`product.value_counts()` shows four reviews for each product. `rating.value_counts().sort_index()` counts each rating and then orders the result by the rating label: there are two reviews each at ratings 1, 2, 3, and 4, and four reviews at rating 5.



`value_counts()` is a useful first check for a categorical column. It can reveal misspelled categories, an unexpected class, or a label that appears only a few times in a large dataset. Run it when getting to know a new dataset.
