### Word2Vec learns by scoring word pairs

**Word2Vec** is introduced as a Google method from 2013. Its basic training process has four steps; the complete toy implementation follows after the explanations.

#### Step 1: collect real pairs

Move through each sentence. Treat the current word as the **center word**, and each word in its context window as a **neighbor**. Each center/neighbor pair becomes a training example.

For *the pizza was delicious*, example pairs include (`the`, `pizza`), (`the`, `was`), (`pizza`, `the`), (`pizza`, `was`), and (`pizza`, `delicious`).

#### Step 2: turn a pair score into a probability

Start the vectors with random values. For a center vector $v$ and neighbor vector $u$, calculate their dot product, then apply the **sigmoid**:

$$P(\text{real pair}) = \sigma(v \cdot u) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-v \cdot u}}$$

- $v$ holds the center word's numbers.
- $u$ holds the neighbor word's numbers.
- $v \cdot u$ is the pair's score.
- $\sigma$ turns the score into a number between 0 and 1.

| $v \cdot u$ | −2 | 0 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\sigma(v \cdot u)$ | 0.12 | 0.5 | 0.88 |

A large score means the model treats the pair as likely to be real; a negative score makes it less likely. Word2Vec stores two vectors for each word: one when it is the center ($v$), another when it is the neighbor ($u$). After training, the example keeps the center vectors.
