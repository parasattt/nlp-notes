### Why separate word columns cannot find synonyms

`CountVectorizer` and TF-IDF give each vocabulary word its own column. For one word, place a 1 in its own column and zeros elsewhere. This is a **one-hot vector**. The next example gives `sofa`, `couch`, `chair`, `banana`, and `delivery` separate positions.



```python
words5 = ["sofa", "couch", "chair", "banana", "delivery"]
one_hot = {w: np.eye(5)[i] for i, w in enumerate(words5)}
print("sofa  :", one_hot["sofa"])
print("couch :", one_hot["couch"])
print()
print("cosine(sofa, couch)  =", cosine(one_hot["sofa"], one_hot["couch"]))
print("cosine(sofa, banana) =", cosine(one_hot["sofa"], one_hot["banana"]))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
sofa  : [1. 0. 0. 0. 0.]
couch : [0. 1. 0. 0. 0.]

cosine(sofa, couch)  = 0.0
cosine(sofa, banana) = 0.0
```



Different words have their 1 in different positions. Their dot product is therefore 0, and so is their cosine similarity.

In this representation, `sofa` is no closer to `couch` than it is to `banana`. The program needs vectors whose values capture how words are used.
