### Learn from a word's neighbors

People write both *I sat on the sofa* and *I sat on the couch*. Similar surrounding words provide evidence that `sofa` and `couch` have similar uses. The lecture's guiding idea is:

> Words that occur next to the same words tend to have similar meanings.

The program can find this pattern by reading text; it does not need a supplied synonym list.

The toy corpus below has twelve sentences. For each word, count neighbors up to two positions to its left or right. This distance is the **context window**.



The nested loops count words within `WINDOW = 2` on each side of a center word, excluding the center itself. A `defaultdict(Counter)` stores one neighbor-frequency table for each word.

```python
from collections import Counter, defaultdict

toy_text = [
    "the pizza was delicious", "the burger was delicious", "the pizza was cold",
    "the burger was cold", "we ordered a pizza", "we ordered a burger",
    "the waiter was rude", "the waitress was rude", "the waiter was friendly",
    "the waitress was friendly", "the waiter brought the pizza", "the waitress brought the burger",
]
toy = [s.split() for s in toy_text]

WINDOW = 2
neighbours = defaultdict(Counter)                   # neighbours["pizza"]["the"] = how often
for sent in toy:
    for i, word in enumerate(sent):
        for j in range(max(0, i - WINDOW), min(len(sent), i + WINDOW + 1)):
            if j != i:
                neighbours[word][sent[j]] += 1

for w in ["pizza", "burger", "waiter", "waitress"]:
    print(f"{w:9}", dict(sorted(neighbours[w].items())))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
pizza     {'a': 1, 'brought': 1, 'cold': 1, 'delicious': 1, 'ordered': 1, 'the': 3, 'was': 2}
burger    {'a': 1, 'brought': 1, 'cold': 1, 'delicious': 1, 'ordered': 1, 'the': 3, 'was': 2}
waiter    {'brought': 1, 'friendly': 1, 'rude': 1, 'the': 4, 'was': 2}
waitress  {'brought': 1, 'friendly': 1, 'rude': 1, 'the': 4, 'was': 2}
```



#### Check the pizza counts by hand

`pizza` appears in four sentences:

- *the **pizza** was delicious*: neighbors are *the, was, delicious*.
- *the **pizza** was cold*: neighbors are *the, was, cold*.
- *we ordered a **pizza***: neighbors are *ordered, a*. The word *we* is three positions away, outside the window.
- *the waiter brought the **pizza***: neighbors are *brought, the*.

The total is *the* three times, *was* twice, and each other listed neighbor once. This matches the saved table.

`pizza` and `burger` have identical neighbor counts even though they never occur in the same sentence. So do `waiter` and `waitress`. Each row of neighbor counts is already a vector, which the next example compares with cosine similarity.
