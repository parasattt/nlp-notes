### Explore the learned vectors

| Function | Result |
|---|---|
| `most_similar(word)` | Words with the highest cosine similarity to the query. |
| `similarity(a, b)` | Cosine similarity of two words, from −1 to 1. |
| `doesnt_match([...])` | The word that fits least well with the others. |



```python
for word in ["pizza", "burrito", "waiter", "delicious", "rude", "manager", "cheap"]:
    near = ", ".join(f"{w} {s:.2f}" for w, s in yelp_vectors.most_similar(word, topn=6))
    print(f"{word:10} -> {near}")
```

The saved neighbors show that the model learned several useful patterns from reviews:

- **Synonyms:** `waiter` is near `server` and `waitress`; `delicious` is near `yummy` and `tasty`.
- **Related dishes:** `burrito` is near `asada`, `quesadilla`, and other Mexican dishes.
- **Misspellings and short forms:** `cheep` is near `cheap`; `mgr` and `gm` are near `manager`.
- A warning sign: `expensive` is also a neighbor of `cheap`.

### Similar context does not mean the same sentiment

`good`/`bad` has a similarity almost as high as `good`/`great`. As with `delicious`/`cold`, both words can appear in *the food was ___*.

These vectors can capture what a word is **about**, such as food, staff, or price, without clearly separating positive and negative opinions. Query expansion can therefore make mistakes—for example, adding `expensive` when the user searches for `cheap`.
