### Train with gensim

The training call exposes these settings:

| Setting | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `vector_size` | 100 | Values stored in each word vector. |
| `window` | 5 | How far left/right to look for neighbors. |
| `min_count` | 5 | Skip words seen fewer than five times because they have too few examples to learn from. |
| `sg` | 1 | Predict neighbors from the center word, as in the toy example. |
| `negative` | 5 | Fake pairs added per real pair. |
| `epochs` | 5 | Passes over the training text. |
| `workers` | 2 | Processor cores used for training. |

Settings chosen before training are **hyperparameters**. With two workers, work can be divided slightly differently between runs, so results can vary even with the source's seed setting. The lecture estimates about a minute in Colab; the saved run below records its own timing.



```python
from gensim.models import Word2Vec

start = time.time()
w2v = Word2Vec(train_sents, vector_size=100, window=5, min_count=5,
               sg=1, negative=5, epochs=5, workers=2, seed=0)
print(f"trained in {time.time() - start:.0f} s, vocabulary {len(w2v.wv):,} words")
print("vector of 'pizza' (first 10 of 100 numbers):", w2v.wv["pizza"][:10])
```


**Saved output:**

```text

vector of 'pizza' (first 10 of 100 numbers): [-0.45  0.14 -0.24 -0.63  0.59 -0.35 -0.07 -0.43  0.36 -0.11]
```



The individual vector positions do not have fixed labels such as “food” or “positive.” The useful information comes from comparing vectors, for example with cosine similarity.
