## Subwords in Kazakh



The multilingual `xlm-roberta-base` tokenizer processes English, Russian and Kazakh examples. Read the leading `▁` as a word-start marker.

```python
xlmr = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("xlm-roberta-base")

print("EN:", xlmr.tokenize("tokenization unhappiness"))
print("RU:", xlmr.tokenize("ноутбука покупкам"))
print("KK:", xlmr.tokenize("кітаптарымызда дүкеннен сатып алдым"))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
EN: ['▁to', 'ken', 'ization', '▁unha', 'ppi', 'ness']
RU: ['▁ноутбук', 'а', '▁покупка', 'м']
KK: ['▁кітап', 'тар', 'ымыз', 'да', '▁', 'дүк', 'ен', 'нен', '▁сатып', '▁алды', 'м']
```



Look at the saved Kazakh split:

```text
кітаптарымызда -> ▁кітап  тар  ымыз  да
```

The lecture reads these pieces as `кітап` (book) + `тар` (plural) + `ымыз` (our) + `да` (locative): “in our books”. `▁` marks the start of a word. The tokenizer learned frequent patterns in text without being given Kazakh grammar rules.

Do not assume every split follows grammar. `дүкеннен` (“from the shop”) becomes `дүк` + `ен` + `нен`, splitting the root `дүкен` in two. Subword tokenizers find **frequent character sequences**, rather than morphemes. In the lecture's Kazakh examples, repeated suffixes sometimes make these units line up.

The lecture argues that representing words as pieces avoids the unknown-word failure of a whole-word vocabulary, and that Kazakh's many forms per root make a whole-word vocabulary difficult. These are the lecture's claims; the saved results show both a useful split and a linguistically incorrect split.
