## Stop words



The code lowercases the review, tokenizes it, retains alphabetic tokens with `isalpha()` and prints the eight most common words.

```python
review = """I bought this laptop for my daughter and she loves it. The screen is bright and the
battery lasts all day. It is not the fastest machine in the world but it is more than enough for
the things that she does with it."""

tokens = word_tokenize(review.lower())
words = [t for t in tokens if t.isalpha()]

print(Counter(words).most_common(8))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
[('the', 5), ('it', 4), ('is', 3), ('for', 2), ('and', 2), ('she', 2), ('i', 1), ('bought', 1)]
```



Words such as `the`, `it`, `is` and `and` tell us little about this review's topic. They are called **stop words**. Removing them can make topic-related word counts easier to read, as in [[EN/Lectures/3 - Parsing Text and Regex/3.13 - Corpus Counts and Stop Words|the earlier corpus-count example]].



An English stop word list is converted to a set for membership checks. The saved output reports 198 items and the first 20 alphabetically.

```python
from nltk.corpus import stopwords

stop_en = set(stopwords.words("english"))

print("how many:", len(stop_en))
print("sample  :", sorted(list(stop_en))[:20])
```


**Saved output:**

```text
how many: 198
sample  : ['a', 'about', 'above', 'after', 'again', 'against', 'ain', 'all', 'am', 'an', 'and', 'any', 'are', 'aren', "aren't", 'as', 'at', 'be', 'because', 'been']
```



Filter the tokens against that set, then compare the remaining words and their eight highest counts.

```python
content = [w for w in words if w not in stop_en]

print(content)
print()
print(Counter(content).most_common(8))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
['bought', 'laptop', 'daughter', 'loves', 'screen', 'bright', 'battery', 'lasts', 'day', 'fastest', 'machine', 'world', 'enough', 'things']

[('bought', 1), ('laptop', 1), ('daughter', 1), ('loves', 1), ('screen', 1), ('bright', 1), ('battery', 1), ('lasts', 1)]
```



The remaining counts now show the review’s subject more clearly: `laptop`, `screen`, `battery` and `day`.
