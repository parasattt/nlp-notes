## Recap

- Keyword rules cannot express how strongly a word supports a class.
- Naive Bayes estimates $P(\text{class})$ and $P(\text{word} \mid \text{class})$ from counts, then combines them with Bayes' rule.
- “Naive” means assuming words are independent for the calculation. This is false for real language, yet the method can still be useful.
- A zero observed word likelihood can erase the whole product. Laplace smoothing, controlled by `alpha`, prevents that.
- Long products can underflow. Log space replaces multiplication with addition; `feature_log_prob_` stores log word likelihoods.
- Fit the vectorizer only on training text. Fitting on test text leaks information into training and can inflate the score.
- Testing on training examples mainly measures fitting known answers and can look perfect.
- Accuracy hides different error costs and class imbalance. The always-ham example reaches 90% accuracy while catching no spam.
- Precision and recall answer different questions. Decide which mistake costs more before evaluating the model.
- Inspect learned evidence. That is how the example reveals a model partly learning formality instead of spam.
