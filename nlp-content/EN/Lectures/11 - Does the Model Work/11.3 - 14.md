# Part 3. Overfitting with words

Notebook 10's word model used `Ridge`. Plain `LinearRegression` is the same model without the
addition Ridge makes. With all words as features (`min_df=1`) there are about 35 000 columns - three
times more weights than the 12 000 training reviews. Train it on growing parts of the training set:

(This cell takes about 15 seconds.)

```python
rows = []
for n in [300, 3000, 12000]:
    vec = TfidfVectorizer().fit(y_train_df["text"][:n])
    Xn, Xt = vec.transform(y_train_df["text"][:n]), vec.transform(y_test_df["text"])
    plain = LinearRegression().fit(Xn, y_train_df["stars"][:n])
    rows.append((n, Xn.shape[1],
                 mae(plain.predict(Xn), y_train_df["stars"][:n]),
                 mae(plain.predict(Xt), y_test_df["stars"])))
print(pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["training reviews", "weights", "train MAE", "test MAE"]).round(3).to_string(index=False))
print("\nbaseline (always the average): 1.173")
```

**Saved output:**

```text
 training reviews  weights  train MAE  test MAE
              300     5123        0.0     0.924
             3000    17683        0.0     0.982
            12000    35020        0.0     1.408

baseline (always the average): 1.173
```

Training error is **0.000** every time: with more weights than examples, the model can always find
weights that hit every training rating exactly - like the degree-7 curve. Test error is large, and
with 12 000 reviews it is 1.41 - **worse than the baseline** that ignores the text entirely.

More weights than examples makes this extreme, but the cause is general: nothing in the loss stops
the weights from growing as large as they like to fit each training review's exact quirks.
