### Measure how surprised the model is

To compare models, score the same **held-out text**—text not used to build their counts. A useful model gives higher probabilities to the words that actually occur.

**Perplexity** combines these word probabilities into one number:

$$\text{perplexity} = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \log P(w_i \mid \text{previous words})\right)$$

- $N$ is the number of scored word steps.
- $w_i$ is the actual word at step $i$.
- $P(w_i\mid\text{previous words})$ is the probability assigned to that word by the model.

Work through the formula:

1. Take the log probability of every real word, $\log P(w_i\mid\dots)$.
2. Add the logs and divide by $N$ to get their average: $\frac{1}{N} \sum$.
3. Change the sign and apply $\exp$ to undo the logarithm.

**Lower is better.** A perplexity of 50 is like being unsure among fifty equally likely words at each step.

- Uniformly choosing among six possible words gives perplexity 6.
- Always knowing the next word gives perplexity 1.
- Treating all 35,000 vocabulary words equally gives perplexity 35,000.

#### Calculate one sentence by hand

For *your order has shipped*, the five step probabilities are $2/5$, $3/4$, $1/3$, 1, and 1. Their product is 0.1. The **geometric mean** is $0.1^{1/5}=0.631$: multiplying that mean by itself five times gives the same product.

Perplexity is its reciprocal: $1/0.631\approx1.58$. In this example, the average uncertainty is like choosing between about 1.6 equally likely words.



`perplexity_toy` sums log probabilities, counts scored transitions, divides by that count, and applies the negative exponential. The three calls compare raw counts, add-one, and uniform probabilities on the same sentence.

```python
def perplexity_toy(sentences, p):
    log_sum, n = 0.0, 0
    for s in sentences:
        words = add_marks(s)
        for prev, word in zip(words, words[1:]):
            log_sum += math.log(p(word, prev))
            n += 1
    return math.exp(-log_sum / n)

s = ["your order has shipped"]
print("no smoothing          :", round(perplexity_toy(s, p_next), 3))
print("add-one smoothing     :", round(perplexity_toy(s, p_add_k), 3))
print("all 13 words equal    :", round(perplexity_toy(s, lambda w, prev: 1 / V), 3))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
no smoothing          : 1.585
add-one smoothing     : 6.309
all 13 words equal    : 13.0
```



The uniform toy model has perplexity exactly 13, equal to the vocabulary size $V$. Add-one smoothing increases the familiar sentence's perplexity to about 6.3 because it takes probability away from its observed pairs.

On new sentences, smoothing can help or hurt. Held-out text is the comparison that matters, so the lecture next evaluates the Yelp models.
