### Estimate the next word from counts

The toy model has seen these five replies:

1. thank you for your order
2. thank you for your patience
3. your order has shipped
4. your order is delayed
5. thank you for contacting us

#### What follows “for”?

`for` occurs three times: twice before `your`, once before `contacting`. Divide a pair's count by the count of the preceding word:

$$P(\text{your} \mid \text{for}) = \frac{\text{count}(\text{for your})}{\text{count}(\text{for})} = \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.67 \qquad P(\text{contacting} \mid \text{for}) = \frac{1}{3}$$

$P(\text{your}\mid\text{for})$ means “the probability of `your`, given the previous word `for`.” The bar $\mid$ means **given**. The numerator counts the pair; the denominator counts opportunities after the preceding word.

A **bigram** contains two neighboring words. A **trigram** contains three. These counts describe the supplied replies, not every possible English sentence.

The same sequence units are used as text features in [[EN/Lectures/6 - Turning Text into Features/6.10 - N-grams and Negation Features|n-gram features]], though here their counts estimate next-word probabilities.
