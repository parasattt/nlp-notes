### Longer contexts are much harder to observe

Even after training on about six million words, **9.5% of test pairs** and **36.5% of test triples** were never seen in training.

An unseen triple receives only the artificial $k$ contribution in the add-k trigram model. Two-word contexts can make more specific predictions when observed, but they also create many more unseen combinations.

This shortage of observations is **data sparsity**. The source emphasizes that more data alone does not remove the growth problem: each extra context word multiplies possible combinations by vocabulary size $V$.

Keep a way to use shorter contexts: backoff for suggestions, or interpolation for probability estimates.
