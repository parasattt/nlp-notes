## Exercises

**1. More replies.**

Add three replies of your own to `replies` (for example *your refund is processed*, *thank you for
your feedback*, *your order is on its way*) and run the cells of Part 1 again.

1. By hand first, then in code: what is $P(\text{order} \mid \text{your})$ now?
2. Compute `sentence_prob("your order is shipped")` without smoothing and with add-one. Did your new
   replies remove the zero? Why or why not?
3. What is $V$ now? Compute $P(\text{delayed} \mid \text{is})$ with add-one by hand and check it in
   code.

---

**2. Choose the settings properly.**

In Part 5 the values of $k$ and the interpolation weights were compared on the test set.

1. Take the last 5 000 of the training reviews as a **validation** set and count only from the
   first 40 000.
2. On the validation set, try $k$ = 0.001, 0.003, 0.01, 0.03 for the bigram model and at least five
   sets of weights $(l_1, l_2, l_3)$ for interpolation. Keep the best of each.
3. Compute the test perplexity of the two winners once. Are they better than in Part 5 (212 and
   151)? Explain why the answer can be "no" even though you tuned them.

---

**3. A reusable autocomplete.**

1. Write a class `Autocomplete` with `__init__(self, sentences, min_count=2, top_k=4)` that builds
   the two tables (top `top_k` only) and a method `suggest(self, typed)`. Typed words that are not in
   the vocabulary must become `<unk>` before looking them up.
2. Add `save(path)` and `load(path)` with `pickle`. How big is the file, and how long does loading
   take?
3. Write five `assert` checks: empty input, a pair of words that was seen, a pair that was not seen
   (it must fall back), a word not in the vocabulary, and that `suggest` never returns `<s>`.
