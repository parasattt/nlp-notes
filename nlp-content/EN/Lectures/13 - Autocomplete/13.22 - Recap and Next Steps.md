### Recap

- A language model assigns a probability to the next word. A count-based estimate is $P(w\mid\text{prev})=\text{count}(\text{prev }w)/\text{count}(\text{prev})$. The implementation uses dictionaries of `Counter`s.
- `<s>` and `</s>` mark boundaries; `<unk>` represents rare or unseen words.
- Sentence probability combines step probabilities through the **chain rule**. A **Markov assumption** keeps only one or two previous words to make counting practical.
- Unseen pairs otherwise receive zero. Add-one smoothing can give away too much probability: the Yelp bigram's 685 is worse than the unigram's 625. Smaller add-k (212), backoff, and interpolation (151) work better in the supplied experiment.
- Probability products can underflow. Add logs instead: $\log(a\cdot b)=\log a+\log b$.
- **Perplexity** measures average uncertainty on held-out text; lower is better.
- **Data sparsity** is severe: 36.5% of test triples were unseen. Keep a shorter-context fallback.
- Measure both memory and speed, and keep only the suggestions the interface displays. Preserve original totals if displaying probabilities after pruning.
- Counting treats related words as separate keys; word vectors address that missing connection.
