### Products can become too small to store

If twenty word steps each have probability about 0.01, their product is $0.01^{20}=10^{-40}$. For a 400-word text, the product becomes much smaller. The next code compares these products with a sum of logarithms.



```python
print("0.01 ** 20  =", 0.01 ** 20)
print("0.01 ** 400 =", 0.01 ** 400)
print("sum of 400 logs =", 400 * math.log(0.01))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
0.01 ** 20  = 1.0000000000000003e-40
0.01 ** 400 = 0.0
sum of 400 logs = -1842.0680743952364
```



The saved `0.01 ** 400` is exactly **0.0**. The true positive value is too small for the floating-point representation, so it **underflows**. If every long text gets zero, their scores cannot be compared.

Use the identity:

$$\log(a \cdot b) = \log a + \log b$$

Here, $a$ and $b$ are probabilities. Instead of multiplying many small values, add their logarithms. The source's sum is about −1842, which can be stored normally.

For probabilities, logs are zero or negative: $\log 1=0$, $\log0.5=-0.69$, and $\log0.01=-4.6$. A log probability closer to 0 is more likely. The lecture uses log sums for its later language-model evaluation.

The numerical idea is also used in [[EN/Lectures/7 - Naive Bayes Classification/7.5 - Log Probabilities and Underflow|log probabilities for classification]].
