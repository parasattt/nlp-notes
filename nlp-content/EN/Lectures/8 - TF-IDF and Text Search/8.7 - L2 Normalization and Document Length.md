## Compare content without rewarding repetition

Picture a document vector as an arrow. With only `battery` and `delivery` as vocabulary features, the coordinates are how much of each feature the document contains. That gives a two-dimensional picture we can inspect.



The original plotting code draws the four example document arrows and labels their coordinates. The saved figure is retained below.

```python
arrows = [
    # label, (battery, delivery), colour, where to put the label
    ("A twice", (4, 0), "C0", (3.3, 0.12)),
    ("A", (2, 0), "C0", (1.9, 0.12)),
    ("B", (1, 1), "C1", (1.05, 1.05)),
    ("C", (0, 1), "C2", (0.08, 1.05)),
]

plt.figure(figsize=(5.5, 3.6))
for label, (x, y), color, (tx, ty) in arrows:
    plt.annotate("", xy=(x, y), xytext=(0, 0),
                 arrowprops=dict(arrowstyle="->", color=color, lw=2.5 if label == "A twice" else 2))
    plt.text(tx, ty, label, color=color, fontsize=11, weight="bold")
plt.text(2.2, 1.45, "A: battery died, battery useless\n"
                    "A twice: the same review written twice\n"
                    "B: battery ok, delivery slow\n"
                    "C: delivery was late", fontsize=8, va="top")
plt.xlim(-0.2, 4.6); plt.ylim(-0.2, 1.6)
plt.xlabel("count of 'battery'"); plt.ylabel("count of 'delivery'")
plt.title("Documents as arrows")
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```

The saved picture separates direction from length:

- **A** is `(2, 0)` and **A twice** is `(4, 0)`. Repeating the same complaint doubles the arrow's length but keeps its direction. The content proportions have not changed.
- **C**, `(0, 1)`, is at a right angle to A: these two documents share no feature.
- **B**, `(1, 1)`, contains both topics and points between A and C.

To compare content, compare directions instead of rewarding a document just for being longer. **L2 normalisation** scales each nonzero document vector to length 1. It keeps the proportions between features while removing the overall scale.

In the next example, raw counts total 3 for `broke after a week` and 6 when that phrase is repeated. The default TF-IDF vectors are identical after normalisation. The single-letter `a` does not count as a token under the vectorizer defaults.

To normalise a nonzero vector, find its length, then divide every coordinate by that same length. For L2 length, square the coordinates, add them, and take the square root. A has length 2, while A twice has length 4. Dividing `(2, 0)` by 2 and `(4, 0)` by 4 gives `(1, 0)` in both cases. That is why repeating the document does not change its normalised direction.



```python
tv_reviews = TfidfVectorizer()
tv_reviews.fit(reviews)

short = "broke after a week"
long = "broke after a week broke after a week"

cv_raw = CountVectorizer().fit(reviews)
print("counts, short:", cv_raw.transform([short]).toarray().sum())
print("counts, long :", cv_raw.transform([long]).toarray().sum())
print()
a = tv_reviews.transform([short]).toarray()
b = tv_reviews.transform([long]).toarray()
print("tf-idf vectors identical:", np.allclose(a, b))
```
