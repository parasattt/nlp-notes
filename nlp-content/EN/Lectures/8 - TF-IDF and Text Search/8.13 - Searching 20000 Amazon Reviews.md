## Search the larger corpus with the same steps

The algorithm stays the same: fit a TF-IDF vectorizer on the searchable corpus, store its matrix, transform a query, calculate cosine similarities, and sort. Only the documents have changed.

`search_amazon` also times the scoring and sorting and prints short excerpts from the top three matches. Keep these as saved results; no download or search was run during note conversion.

The efficient storage idea is explained in [[EN/Lectures/6 - Turning Text into Features/6.5 - Sparse Matrices and Memory|Sparse Matrices and Memory]].



```python
import time

amazon_tv = TfidfVectorizer()
amazon_matrix = amazon_tv.fit_transform(amazon["text"])
print("vocabulary size:", len(amazon_tv.get_feature_names_out()))

def search_amazon(query, k=3):
    start = time.time()
    scores = cosine_similarity(amazon_tv.transform([query]), amazon_matrix)[0]
    top = scores.argsort()[::-1][:k]
    print(f"QUERY: {query}   ({(time.time() - start) * 1000:.0f} ms over {len(scores)} reviews)")
    for i in top:
        print(f"  {scores[i]:.3f}  [{amazon['sentiment'][i]}]  {amazon['text'][i][:110]}...")
    print()

search_amazon("the screen is cracked")
search_amazon("battery died after two days")
search_amazon("battery died quickly")
```

The saved vocabulary contains 49,848 features. Search takes about 19–21 milliseconds across 20,000 reviews in these stored examples, using a sparse feature matrix and similarity calculation without a separate database or search index.

Read the results as a support agent would:

- `battery died after two days` finds three complaints about devices dying within weeks. The rare, specific word `died` helps make these useful matches.
- `the screen is cracked` finds a cracked colander and a cracked CD case. The distinctive word `cracked` dominates, but this mixed-product corpus contains many kinds of cracked objects. The positive screen review has the right topic and the opposite opinion.
- For `battery died quickly`, the second result is labelled positive but complains about its battery: `everything is doog but battery`, including the original typo. A star rating labels the whole review, not every sentence, so real labels can be noisy.

These are the same strengths and limitations seen on 120 reviews. A larger corpus gives more chances to find the relevant word, and more chances for that word to appear with a different meaning or opinion.
