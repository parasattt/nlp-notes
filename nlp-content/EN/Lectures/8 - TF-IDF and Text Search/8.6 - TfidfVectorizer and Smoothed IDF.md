## Smoothed IDF keeps a weak signal

The library gives `the` an IDF of `1.0`. The notebook presents this default formula:

$$\text{idf}(w) = \log \frac{1 + N}{1 + \text{df}(w)} + 1$$

The variables have the same meanings as before: `N` is the document count and `df(w)` counts documents containing `w`.

1. The `+1` inside the fraction acts as if one extra document contained every vocabulary word. This smooths the counts and prevents division by zero.
2. The `+1` outside the log keeps even an everywhere-present word's IDF at 1 instead of zero. Common words become weaker than rare ones, but are not deleted.

For these three documents, `the` gets `log(4/4) + 1 = 1`. `battery` gets `log(4/3) + 1 ≈ 1.288`. Words in one document get `log(4/2) + 1 ≈ 1.693`, matching the saved IDF dictionary.

The notebook explains this as a choice to keep a weak signal instead of silently discarding a word. That is the same concern raised by stop lists: a frequently used word can still carry useful meaning.



The notebook supplies `TfidfVectorizer(smooth_idf=False, norm=None)` as a way to switch off smoothing and normalisation. Keep the two choices separate: `smooth_idf` concerns the IDF calculation; `norm=None` disables vector normalisation.

The source describes this as switching off “both behaviours,” but it does not show an output for that setting. Do not use this sentence as proof that the snippet exactly reproduces the earlier textbook `log(N/df)` table, or that it removes every `+1` in the displayed library formula. The saved tables here show the defaults. The next topic explains what normalisation changes.

The wider lesson is useful: when your arithmetic differs from a library's result, look for choices in the formula and settings before assuming the library is wrong.
