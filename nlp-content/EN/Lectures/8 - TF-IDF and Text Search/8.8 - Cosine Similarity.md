## Cosine similarity

Raw counts make the repeated review look twice as large. After normalisation, the two reviews are the same vector: they point in the same direction.

**Cosine similarity** measures the angle between document arrows. A value of 1 means the same direction; 0 means a right angle and no shared weighted features. For vectors already scaled to length 1, their dot product gives this similarity directly: multiply matching coordinates and add the products.

Cosine can reach -1 for vectors pointing in opposite directions. TF-IDF features are nonnegative, so the similarities in these examples stay between 0 and 1.

The code below compares three review vectors in a matrix. Row `i`, column `j` is the similarity of review `i` with review `j`; each diagonal entry is a review compared with itself.



```python
from sklearn.metrics.pairwise import cosine_similarity

pair = tv_reviews.transform([
    "the battery died after a week",
    "disappointed the battery died within a week",
    "great gift, my sister was delighted",
])

print(cosine_similarity(pair).round(3))
```


**Saved output:**

```text
[[1.    0.754 0.   ]
 [0.754 1.    0.   ]
 [0.    0.    1.   ]]
```



The two battery complaints have similarity `0.754`: they share `battery`, `died`, and `week`. Each has self-similarity 1.

The gift review shares no weighted words with either complaint, giving similarity 0. It is like the right angle between A and C in the arrow picture.
