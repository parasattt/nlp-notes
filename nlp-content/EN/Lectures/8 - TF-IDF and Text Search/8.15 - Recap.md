## Recap

- Raw-count search can give common words too much influence, tying a cracked-screen complaint with positive reviews about unrelated things.
- Document frequency counts documents containing a word. IDF uses $\log(N / \text{df})$ to give rarer words more weight, while the log limits the size of the gap.
- In this lecture, TF is a word count. TF-IDF multiplies that count by IDF, weakening widespread words and emphasising distinctive ones.
- The default `TfidfVectorizer` uses smoothed IDF and an outer `+1`, so common vocabulary words keep a nonzero weight. It also uses L2 normalisation so repeating the same document does not make its vector bigger.
- Normalisation keeps an arrow's direction while removing length. Cosine similarity compares directions and provides a simple document-search score.
- Matching words does not mean understanding meanings or opinions: `died quickly` can match `arrived quickly`.
- TF-IDF can weaken `not` without removing it. That avoids the meaning lost by a stop list that deletes negation.
- IDF depends on the corpus. For classification, learn it only from training data.
- Character n-grams can match `battery` with `batery`, but can also pull `happy` toward `unhappy`.
- The same core search steps scale from 120 teaching reviews to the saved examples of 20,000 Amazon reviews.
