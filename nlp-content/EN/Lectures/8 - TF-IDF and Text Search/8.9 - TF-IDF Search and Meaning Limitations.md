## Search with TF-IDF and cosine similarity

We can now build a search engine: transform a query with the same fitted TF-IDF vectorizer, compare its direction with every stored review vector, and return the largest similarities. The source describes this basic idea, with refinements, as an approach used in document search for decades.

The function below uses `k=3` by default. It sorts the scores in descending order and prints the top review texts together with their labels. The labels are shown for inspection; they are not used to compute similarity.



```python
review_matrix = tv_reviews.transform(reviews)

def search(query, k=3):
    scores = cosine_similarity(tv_reviews.transform([query]), review_matrix)[0]
    for i in scores.argsort()[::-1][:k]:
        print(f"  {scores[i]:.3f}  [{review_labels[i]}]  {reviews[i]}")

for q in ["the screen is cracked", "broke after a week", "comfortable and quiet"]:
    print("QUERY:", q)
    search(q)
    print()
```


**Saved output:**

```text
QUERY: the screen is cracked
  0.507  [negative]  Recieved it with a cracked screen and the seller refused a refund
  0.311  [negative]  Not good. The screen flickers constantly.
  0.248  [positive]  Light, fast, sharp screen. Love it!!!

QUERY: broke after a week
  0.403  [negative]  do not buy this it stopped working after a week
  0.348  [negative]  It looked nice but broke after 3 uses
  0.317  [negative]  terrible quality broke after two days waste of money

QUERY: comfortable and quiet
  0.318  [positive]  Impressed by how quiet it is
  0.291  [positive]  Comfortable to wear all day. No complaints.
  0.066  [negative]  poor quality and slow delivery very disappointed

```



The cracked-screen complaint now ranks first at `0.507`; the flickering-screen complaint is next at `0.311`. Common words such as `the` and `is` still contribute, but the sound-quality and colour reviews no longer tie with the cracked screen.

The third result is positive praise for a sharp screen. It is on the right **topic** but has a different **opinion**. TF-IDF similarity does not understand sentiment just because the displayed results have sentiment labels.

This is still useful for finding earlier support messages on a related topic. The next query shows the limit more clearly.



```python
print("QUERY: battery died quickly")
search("battery died quickly")
```


**Saved output:**

```text
QUERY: battery died quickly
  0.564  [negative]  disappointed the battery died within a week
  0.222  [positive]  great value for money and it arrived very quickly
  0.220  [positive]  very satisfied the battery lasts all day highly recommend
```



The battery complaint is correctly first. The next two results are positive: one says delivery `arrived very quickly`, and one praises a battery that lasts all day. Each shares a useful-looking word with the query.

TF-IDF matches words rather than meanings. It cannot tell that `quickly` after `died` describes a problem, while `quickly` after `arrived` describes good delivery. The source points forward to word embeddings as a first attempt to address this limitation.
